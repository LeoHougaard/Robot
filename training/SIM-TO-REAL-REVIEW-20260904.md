**What Leo is trying to build.** The consistent intent across the threads is one policy that walks in different places, follows forward/sideways/turn commands, and eventually combines them with crouching and body roll/pitch. It should tolerate imperfect measurements, payload and ground changes, use motor current when it helps, and run on the Pixel with minimal supervision. Leo operates the physical robot. Requests to increase penalties, extend episodes, change stiffness, or simplify to walking were attempts to escape repeated failure; they should be treated as experimental suggestions, not permanent requirements overriding that outcome. Keep the dominant objective body tracking, with only a subordinate, progress-gated diagonal pairing prior. Use mild terrain variety from the beginning, then increase difficulty as skill improves. Recovery remains a separate project/stage.

**Why this direction is supported by research.** Quadruped transfer research explicitly identifies actuator behavior and latency as major parts of closing the simulation gap. Randomization improves robustness around a credible model; it cannot establish that an incorrect joint transform is correct. This supports measuring Leo's servo/linkage chain before another major training investment. [Tan et al., 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.10332). Work on ANYmal also demonstrates the importance of representing actuator dynamics for transfer; its actuator and robot differ from this project's hobby servos. [Hwangbo et al., 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.08652).

Performance-based terrain progression and careful treatment of timeouts are established approaches. The often quoted minutes-to-walk results used thousands of environments and a different robot/simulation workload; they are not a time estimate for this closed-linkage model on GB10. [Rudin et al., 2021/2022](https://arxiv.org/html/2109.11978v3).

Recent sensor/action history can help infer changing conditions such as payload and ground behavior. RMA demonstrates one approach to this adaptation, but does not establish that a bigger network or its exact architecture is best here. Start with the existing compact history actor and compare alternatives only after the measurement and timing contract is credible. [Kumar et al., 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.04034).

A policy using only body/joint/current sensing can react to ground contact. It cannot observe an upcoming gap before contact. Anticipating larger obstacles is a separate perception problem; add camera/depth information later if the required terrain demands it. This is a scope boundary, not a promise that blind walking will handle every surface. [Miki et al., 2022](https://leggedrobotics.github.io/rl-perceptiveloco/).

**The proposed training system.** Keep PPO as the learning algorithm initially. The deployed actor is the small network that chooses servo targets from available sensor history and commands. A training-only critic can additionally see exact simulated velocity, contacts, terrain and randomized physical parameters, helping judge actions without exposing those unavailable signals to the deployed actor. This asymmetric critic is a proposed experiment, not an implemented or measured improvement in this repository. The active configuration currently has no separate privileged state.

Use one new environment with explicit, saved experiment configurations instead of adding another long chain of version inheritance. Archive V1 through V19 and their resolved configurations for reproduction. Freeze an actor input/output contract: joint ordering, coordinate transforms, units, normalization, estimator state, sample timestamps, history order, command smoothing, action filtering, slew limits, target hold and reset behavior. Derive deployment metadata from the resolved training configuration. Changing this contract means a new policy version, not silently reinterpreting an old checkpoint.

Model the complete path from a command to measured motion. Start with a supported leg/robot and record safe trajectories in both directions, at several amplitudes, speeds and representative loads. Log requested and acknowledged target, encoder angle, current/validity, voltage, firmware timestamps, and synchronized video. Fit delay, response, deadband, saturation and speed under load with uncertainty. Reserve entire trajectories for validation; do not randomly split neighboring samples from the same gait and call that independent validation. A gait's p95 velocity is an observation, not a servo specification. Avoid stacking response filters that count the same physical delay twice.

Before those fits, verify the geometric contract with known poses and small independent servo motions: the same encoder vector must produce the same leg pose and foot direction in CAD/simulation and runtime. Check masses, inertias, assembled center of mass, battery/phone payload, joint limits, foot collision geometry and reachable step height. A detailed CAD mesh or passing joint-anchor test alone does not validate dynamics. If full linkage simulation is the throughput bottleneck, benchmark a reduced model against linkage kinematics and loaded trajectories before using it for training; retain the full model for final comparison.

The simulator should generate raw sampled sensors and run the same causal preprocessing as Pixel. Vary actual action hold/delivery and sensor age, rather than independently randomizing a reported interval. Include finite-difference velocity noise, orientation-estimator behavior, delayed/repeated packets, current dropout and startup history. The actor must not receive perfect body velocity or terrain height unless those are actually estimated on the phone. Training rewards and critic state may use simulator truth.

For learning, score closeness to every requested body component, including unwanted motion on zero-target axes. Stillness with zero motion command must be a useful outcome; posture-only commands must permit stabilization. Separate a true fall from an ordinary episode timeout and test that ending an episode does not reward failure. Keep the body objective dominant and diagonal pairing subordinate to actual progress. Add other penalties only when a measured failure warrants them, rather than accumulating style rewards.

Start with mostly achievable low-speed commands, stops, turns and small mixed posture changes on mild varied terrain. Broaden command ranges, terrain, disturbances and physical uncertainty when held-out tests show progress; retain easy cases to detect forgotten skills. Randomize around measured plausible ranges, including linked effects such as voltage and available drive. Large arbitrary randomness can make the task physically impossible rather than robust. Separately train a no-current baseline and a current-enabled actor at matched budgets/seeds, then test delayed, biased, shuffled and missing current. Keep current only when that evidence supports its contribution.

Longer episodes do not preserve learning that would otherwise be lost: resetting an episode changes the simulated robot state, not the learned network weights. Episode duration, PPO rollout length, and discount horizon are different. With gamma 0.99 at 50 Hz, rewards decay by a factor of e over roughly two seconds, even with a 90-second episode. Review these together and preserve correct timeout bootstrapping rather than using a longer timeout as the main remedy.

**Visual guidance and acceptance.** Leo explicitly asked for visual observation to guide training. At each evaluation milestone, render the exact checkpoint being measured, with its hash, profile, fit, terrain/seed and command script recorded. Use a close side/front view for feet, an elevated view for body motion, and a fixed scene view for actual distance and obstacle traversal. Include the command and elapsed time in review evidence. Keep media silent.

Compare the same fixed cases before changing training: stop/start, forward/reverse, sideways, yaw-only, mixed turn/walk, posture while stationary and moving, mild slopes/roughness, then steps and disturbances within the robot's measured capability. Retain a separate held-out set of terrain seeds and physical parameter combinations. Test enough distance to leave the central spawn platform. A video containing stairs is not evidence of climbing them.

Classify each failure from video and telemetry: stepping without progress, sliding, limited target response, knee/hip coordination mismatch, body tilt/collapse, command transitions, obstacle contact, or timing/current corruption. Record observation separately from suspected cause. Make one general change to test the leading cause, rerun the same cases, and promote/reject/mark inconclusive. Contact sheets help triage, but rapid stumbles and impacts require the underlying video/denser frames. Rendering should occur between training windows or on a separately verified compatible resource, not compete blindly with the active run.

Promotion requires passing the existing deterministic numerical gates, completing the intended route without resets, and visual confirmation that the measured motion is useful locomotion. Add posture/mixed-command cases before claiming six-command control: the present CurrentBody locomotion gate is deliberately narrower. Record speed/yaw/posture errors, falls, distance, action saturation, slip/contact behavior, current validity, and loop timing; reward alone never promotes a policy. Fix the remaining provenance gap so normal and stress results identify their distinct conditions and the exact exported actor.

Then run golden recorded raw-sensor sequences through Python and Kotlin/ONNX and compare observations, actions, transformed servo targets, and history reset behavior. An actor-only random-vector comparison misses preprocessing mistakes. Benchmark the entire Pixel-to-firmware feedback cycle under load with motors disabled before supervised physical tests. Leo then performs supported standing, brief flat walking, start/stop/turns and progressively rougher surfaces, with logs/video used to update model uncertainty and retrain. Physical success remains an observed hardware outcome.
